Article Title:
 Tasmania: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander population summary

Article Content:
In Tasmania 30,000 people identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander in the 2021 Census of Population and Housing. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people represented 5.4% of Tasmania’s population. This was up from 4.6% in 2016, and 4.0% in 2011.
Of the 30,000 people who identified as being of Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander origin:
  91.9% identified as Aboriginal
  4.1% identified as Torres Strait Islander
  4.1% identified as both Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander.
The median age for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in Tasmania increased over the last ten years. In 2021 the median age was 25 years, up from 24 years in 2016 and 22 years in 2011.
In 2021, 49.2% of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in Tasmania were aged under 25 years, down from 54.4% in 2011.
The proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 75 years and over in 2021 was larger than in 2011 (2.3% compared to 1.5%).
In 2021, the Tasmanian Local Government Area (LGA) with the most Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people was Launceston, followed by Glenorchy and Clarence.
In the Launceston LGA Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people represented:
  4.1% of the LGA population
  9.5% of the overall Tasmanian Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander population.
The top five LGAs with the greatest proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people were:
  Circular Head (17.3% of the total LGA population)
  Flinders (Tas.) (16.2%)
  Brighton (11.6%)
  Huon Valley (10.2%)
  Waratah Wynyard (8.8%).
In the Census, an Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander household is a  where at least one person identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander. On Census Night the person needs to have been present and a usual resident at the dwelling.
The average size of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in Tasmania was 2.9 people, down from 3.0 people in 2011.
Most Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households (79.6%) were family households, including 2.7% with more than one family living together. One in six households (16.0%) were made up of people who lived alone while a small proportion were group households (4.5%).

The most common Tenure types () for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in Tasmania were:
  owned with a mortgage (35.2%)
  owned outright (20.2%)
  rented through a real estate agent (15.1%)
  rented through a state or territory housing authority (11.3%).
In 2021, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households had a median:
  weekly rent () of $265
  monthly mortgage () of $1,300.

  most (96.4%) used only English at home ()

One third (32.7%) of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in Tasmania reported an equivalised total household weekly income () of $1,000 or more in 2021, compared with 36.7% nationally.
The median equivalised total household weekly income for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in Tasmania was $768. This was lower than the national average ($830).
The proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 20 to 24 years in Tasmania who had completed Year 12 or equivalent as their highest year of school was 56.2%, up from 36.3% in 2011.
Almost one in ten (9.3%) people aged 18 24 years were attending university or other higher education institutions at the time of the 2021 Census, up from 6.7% in 2011.
In 2021, 2.4% of people aged 25 years and over were attending university or other higher education institutions, up from 2.0% in 2011.
