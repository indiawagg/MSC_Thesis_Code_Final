Article Title:
 Northern Territory: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander population summary

Article Content:
In the Northern Territory 61,000 people identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander in the 2021 Census of Population and Housing. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people represented 26.3% of the Northern Territory population. This was up from 25.5% in 2016, and down from 26.8% in 2011.
Of the 61,000 people who identified as being of Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander origin:
  95.8% identified as Aboriginal
  1.1% identified as Torres Strait Islander
  3.0% identified as both Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander.
The median age for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in the Northern Territory increased over the last 10 years. In 2021 the median age was 26 years, up from 25 years in 2016 and 23 years in 2011.
In 2021, 46.8% of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in the Northern Territory were aged under 25 years, down from 52.5% in 2011.
The proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 75 years and over in 2021 was the same as 2011 (1.2%).
In 2021, the Northern Territory Local Government Area (LGA) with the most Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people was East Arnhem, followed by Darwin and Alice Springs.
In the East Arnhem LGA, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people represented:
  89.9% of the LGA population
  12.9% of the overall Northern Territory Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander population.
The top five LGAs with the greatest proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people were:
  Belyuen (100% of the total LGA population)
  East Arnhem (89.9%)
  West Daly (88.7%)
  Tiwi Islands (86.5%)
  MacDonnell (85.6%).
In the Census, an Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander household is a  where at least one person identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander. On Census Night the person needs to have been present and a usual resident at the dwelling.
The average size of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in the Northern Territory was 3.8 people, down from 4.2 people in 2011.
Most Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households (81.9%) were family households, including 17.0% with more than one family living together. One in seven households (14.5%) were made up of people who lived alone while a small proportion were group households (3.6%).

The most common Tenure types () for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in the Northern Territory were:
  rented through a state or territory housing authority (35.4%)
  owned with a mortgage (16.1%)
  rented through a community housing provider (13.0%)
  rented through a real estate agent (11.8%).
In 2021, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households had a median:
  weekly rent () of $130
  monthly mortgage () of $2,000.

Just over one quarter (28.5%) of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in the Northern Territory reported an equivalised total household weekly income () of $1,000 or more in 2021, compared with 36.7% nationally.
The median equivalised total household weekly income for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in the Northern Territory was $578. This was lower than the national average of $830.
The proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 20 to 24 years in the Northern Territory who had completed Year 12 or equivalent as their highest year of school was 33.6%, up from 17.8% in 2011.
In 2021. 2.3% of people aged 18 24 years were attending university or other higher education institutions, up from 1.8% in 2011.
The proportion of those aged 25 years and over attending university or other higher education institutions did not change between 2021 and 2011 (1.3%).
