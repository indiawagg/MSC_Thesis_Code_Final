Article Title:
 New South Wales: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander population summary

Article Content:
In New South Wales 278,000 people identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander in the 2021 Census of Population and Housing. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people represented 3.4% of the New South Wales population. This is up from 2.9% in 2016, and 2.5% in 2011.
Of the 278,000 people who identified as being of Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander origin:
  96.1% identified as Aboriginal
  1.8% identified as Torres Strait Islander
  2.1% identified as both Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander.
The median age for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in New South Wales increased over the last ten years. In 2021 the median age was 23 years, up from 22 years in 2016, and 21 years in 2011.
In 2021, 51.8% of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in New South Wales were aged under 25 years, down from 55.6% in 2011.
The proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 75 years and over in 2021 was larger than in 2011 (2.0% compared to 1.3%).
In 2021, the New South Wales Local Government Area (LGA) with the most Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people was the Central Coast (NSW), followed by Blacktown and Lake Macquarie.
In the Central Coast (NSW) LGA Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people represented:
  4.9% of the LGA population
  6.1% of the overall New South Wales Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander population.
The top five areas with the greatest proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people were:
  Brewarrina (51.4% of the total LGA population)
  Central Darling (36.5%)
  Coonamble (33.9%)
  Bourke (30.3%)
  Walgett (21.2%).
In the Census, an Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander household is a  where at least one person identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander. On Census Night, the person needs to have been present and a usual resident at the dwelling.
The average size of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in New South Wales was 3.1 people in both 2021 and 2011.
Most Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households (79.4%) were family households, including 4.4% with more than one family living together. One in seven households (15.4%) were made up of people who lived alone while a small proportion were group households (5.1%).

In 2021, 38.5% of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in New South Wales reported an equivalised total household weekly income () of $1,000 or more in 2021, compared with 36.7% nationally.
The median equivalised total household weekly income for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in New South Wales was $860. This was higher than the national average ($830).
The proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 20 to 24 years in New South Wales who had completed Year 12 or equivalent as their highest year of school was 54.1%, up from 36.7% in 2011.
One in nine (11.5%) people aged 18 24 years were attending university or other higher education institutions at the time of the 2021 Census, up from 8.3% in 2011.
In 2021, 2.6% of people aged 25 years and over were attending university or other higher education institutions, up from 2.3% in 2011.
