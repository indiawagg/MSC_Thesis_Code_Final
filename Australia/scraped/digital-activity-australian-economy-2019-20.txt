Article Title:
 Digital activity in the Australian economy, 2019-20

Article Content:
This article provides an update on the size, growth and nature of digital activity in Australia for 2019 20. These experimental estimates incorporate data from the most recent .

  Digital enabling infrastructure, such as computer hardware, software, telecommunications equipment and support services that form and facilitate the use of computer networks;
  Digital media, which covers digital audio, video and advertisement broadcasting services that can be created, accessed, stored or viewed on digital devices; and
  E commerce, which incorporate retail and wholesale services and margins from digitally ordered or platform enabled online transactions. 
Digital activity value added increased by 7.4% ($7.5 billion) in 2019 20, compared with a 2.0% increase for the total Australian economy. This growth was driven by increases in online sales from retailers and wholesalers.   
Digital activity accounted for 5.9% ($109.0 billion) of total economy value added ($1,857.0 billion), compared to 5.6% ($101.5 billion) in 2018 19 (Graph 1).
In 2019 20, the digital activity share of aggregate value added (5.9%) was higher than 13 industry divisions, but below traditional drivers of economic activity like Mining (10.9%), Finance (8.1%), Construction (7.6%) and Health (7.8%) (Graph 2).
Within digital activity, the share in production was dominated by demand for Support services (44.0%), Telecommunications (21.7%) and Wholesale e commerce (18.9%) (Graph 3).
In 2019 20, the shares of E Commerce   Retail and E Commerce   Wholesale in total digital activity value added increased by 0.8% while the shares of Telecommunications and Digital media decreased by  1.3% and  0.8% respectively.
In 2019 20, digital activity value added grew by 7.4% in current price terms, compared to a 2.0% increase in total economy value added (Graph 4).
Within digital activity, the largest increases were in E commerce   Retail (25.1%) and E commerce   Wholesale (12.3%) driven by growing online trade. Digital media showed a fall of 6.8%, driven by a decrease in free to air radio and TV broadcasting services, which was partially offset by an increase in internet publishing and broadcasting services.
