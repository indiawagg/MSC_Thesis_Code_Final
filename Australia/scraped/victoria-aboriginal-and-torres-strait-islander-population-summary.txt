Article Title:
 Victoria: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander population summary

Article Content:
In Victoria 66,000 people identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander in the 2021 Census of Population and Housing. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people represented 1.0% of Victoria’s population. This was up from 0.8% in 2016, and 0.7% in 2011.
Of the 66,000 people who identified as being of Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander origin:
  94.2% identified as Aboriginal
  3.2% identified as Torres Strait Islander
  2.6% identified as both Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander.
The median age for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in Victoria increased over the last ten years. In 2021 the median age was 24 years, up from 23 years in 2016 and 22 years in 2011.
In 2021, 50.3% of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in Victoria were aged under 25 years, down from 54.7% in 2011.
The proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 75 years and over in 2021 was larger than in 2011 (1.7% compared to 1.5%).
In 2021, the Victorian Local Government Area (LGA) with the most Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people was Greater Geelong, followed by Greater Bendigo and Greater Shepparton.
In the Greater Geelong LGA Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people represented:
  1.3% of the LGA population
  5.4% of the overall Victorian Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander population.
The top five LGAs with the greatest proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people were:
  Mildura (4.6% of the total LGA population)
  Swan Hill (4.5%)
  Greater Shepparton (3.9%)
  East Gippsland (3.5%)
  Wodonga (3.4%).
In the Census, an Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander household is a  where at least one person identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander. On Census Night the person needs to have been present and a usual resident at the dwelling.
The average size of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in Victoria was 3.0 people in both 2021 and 2011.
Most Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households (76.3%) were family households, including 3.4% with more than one family living together. One in six households (16.6%) were made up of people who lived alone while a small proportion were group households (7.0%).

Two in five (39.7%) Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in Victoria reported an equivalised total household weekly income () of $1,000 or more in 2021, compared with 36.7% nationally.
The median equivalised total household weekly income for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander households in Victoria was $882. This was higher than the national average ($830).
The proportion of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people aged 20 to 24 years in Victoria who had completed Year 12 or equivalent as their highest year of school was 60.8%, up from 41.8% in 2011.
One in seven (15.3%) people aged 18 24 years were attending university or other higher education institutions at the time of the 2021 Census, up from 9.9% in 2011.
In 2021, 3.9% of people aged 25 years and over were attending university or other higher education institutions, up from 3.3% in 2011.
