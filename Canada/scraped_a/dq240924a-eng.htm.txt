Date:
2024-09-24
Article Title:
The Daily — Natural resource indicators, second quarter 2024
Article Content:
Released: 2024 09 24
Real gross domestic product (GDP) of the natural resources sector increased 2.0% in the second quarter, after decreasing 0.6% in the first quarter. In comparison, economy wide real GDP rose 0.5% in the second quarter, benefitting from increased domestic demand, and following a rise (+0.4%) in the previous quarter.
The rise in second quarter natural resources real GDP was widespread, with the largest contributors being the energy (+2.6%) and forestry (+1.2%) subsectors. The increase in the energy subsector was driven by a rise in crude oil (+2.7%) that was slightly offset by a decrease in petrochemicals ( 1.0%), the only energy component to decline. Trends in the forestry subsector were more varied with the largest increase among sawmill and wood manufacturing (+5.0%) and the largest decline among pulp and paper manufacturing ( 2.0%). The minerals and mining subsector (+0.7%) recorded the smallest increase overall, as a decrease in non metallic mineral extraction ( 4.1%) largely offset increases in the production of other products.
Despite increases in real GDP, natural resource export volumes decreased 2.1% in the second quarter, following an increase of 1.4% in the previous quarter. The decrease was widespread, with minerals and mining ( 5.8%), forestry ( 4.3%) and hunting, fishing and water ( 2.7%) displaying lower volumes. In the energy subsector (+0.9%), a decrease in refined petroleum products ( 17.3%) export volumes was offset by increases in natural gas (+9.7%) and crude oil (+3.4%).
Import volumes of the natural resources sector were down ( 2.8%) in the second quarter, after increasing slightly (+0.5%) in the previous quarter. Higher import volumes in the minerals and mining (+6.2%) and hunting, fishing and water (+6.1%) subsectors were offset by lower volumes in the energy ( 12.7%) and forestry ( 3.3%) subsectors. The main contributors to the decline were lower imports of refined petroleum products ( 14.7%) and crude oil ( 8.3%).
Natural resource prices rose (+2.7%) in the second quarter, following a decline of 0.5% in the previous quarter. Prices rose across the sector, with minerals and mining displaying the largest increase (+4.5%), as metallic mineral prices (+10.1%) increased. Prices in the energy subsector also rose (+2.4%), influenced by higher values of crude oil (+7.7%). Elsewhere, forestry (+0.8%) and hunting, fishing and water (+0.2%) also contributed to the increase.
In the second quarter, increases in natural resource prices combined with higher real GDP resulted in a 4.7% rise in natural resources nominal GDP. Expressed at an annual rate, natural resources' nominal GDP reached $349.4 billion in the second quarter, representing 12.5% of the Canadian economy.
The number of jobs in the natural resources sector rose slightly in the second quarter, increasing by 0.6%. Increases in the energy (+0.9%), minerals and mining (+0.7%) and hunting, fishing and water (+1.7%) subsectors were the main contributors. These increases were partly offset by decreases in the forestry subsector ( 0.4%).
For analytical purposes, secondary and tertiary processing for the forestry and minerals and mining subsectors are identified separately. The nominal GDP of downstream activities was $10.6 billion in the second quarter, representing a 1.5% decrease. Prices rose slightly (+0.7%) for the second quarter, while they were flat in the first quarter.
