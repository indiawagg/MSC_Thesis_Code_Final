Canada's international transactions in securities, September 2024

Released: 2024-11-18

Foreign investors increased their exposure to Canadian securities by $29.3 billion in September, the largest investment since April. Meanwhile, Canadian acquisitions of foreign securities slowed to $4.1 billion, down from a $12.3 billion investment in August.

As a result, international transactions in securities generated a net inflow of funds of $25.2 billion in the Canadian economy in September, for a total inflow of $30.6 billion in the third quarter.

Strong foreign acquisition of Canadian securities

Foreign investors acquired $29.3 billion of Canadian securities in September, the largest investment since April. The investment in September was mainly in private corporate instruments, both bonds and shares.

Foreign acquisitions of Canadian debt securities totalled $14.7 billion in September, with the bulk of the investment being in bonds. On a year-to-date basis, foreign investors have acquired $172.0 billion of Canadian bonds in 2024, compared with $61.9 billion over the same period in 2023.

Foreign investors added $10.1 billion of Canadian private corporate bonds to their holdings in September, mainly in new bonds by financial institutions and denominated in foreign currencies. They also increased their holdings of federal government bonds by $2.7 billion, the lowest monthly investment in these instruments in 2024.

In September, the Bank of Canada lowered its policy rate to 4.25% from 4.5%. Since the beginning of the year, the rate has been reduced by 75 basis points.

Chart 2 
Foreign investment in Canadian bonds, by sector of issuer

Non-resident investors increased their exposure to Canadian shares by $14.6 billion in September, the largest investment since December 2021. The increase in September 2024 followed a large divestment of $11.9 billion in August. The investment in September was mainly in shares of the energy and mining industry. On a year-to-date basis, foreign divestment in Canadian shares totalled $9.7 billion in 2024, compared with a $40.7 billion divestment over the same period in 2023.

Canadian share prices, as measured by the Standard & Poor's (S&P)/Toronto Stock Exchange composite index, were up by 2.8% in September.

Chart 3 
Foreign investment in Canadian equity and investment fund shares

Canadian investment in foreign securities slows down

Canadian investors acquired $4.1 billion of foreign securities in September, down from $12.3 billion in August. The slowdown in the investment activity was mainly in US shares.

In September, Canadian investors added $4.4 billion of foreign debt securities to their holdings, led by a $6.6 billion investment in bonds. Investment in US government bonds totalled $3.5 billion, following a divestment of $3.9 billion in August.

In contrast, investors reduced their exposure to foreign shares by $296 million in September, following a significant $15.5 billion investment in August. The activity in September was largely related to US equity securities. Canadian investors reduced their holdings of these instruments by $1.2 billion in September, after a $12.7 billion investment in August. US share prices, as measured by the S&P 500 composite index, were up by 2.0% in September.

  Note to readers

The data series on international transactions in securities covers portfolio transactions in equity and investment fund shares, bonds and money market instruments for both Canadian and foreign issues. This activity excludes transactions in equity and debt instruments between affiliated enterprises. These are classified as foreign direct investment in international accounts.

Equity and investment fund shares include common and preferred equities, as well as units or shares of investment funds. For the sake of brevity, the terms "shares" and "equity and investment fund shares" have the same meaning.

Debt securities include bonds and money market instruments.

Bonds have an original term to maturity of more than one year.

Money market instruments have an original term to maturity of one year or less.

Government of Canada paper includes Treasury bills and US-dollar Canada bills.

All values in this release are net transactions unless otherwise stated.
